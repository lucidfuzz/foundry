[ADDED] WORLD-BUILDING STANDARD: EVERY PREFAB, EVERY EDITION
APPROVED intent (Jordan, 12 Oct): "we will be doing this for every single theme, so the rules regarding immersion, world
building, uniqueness, consistency to theme and variety need to be airtight." Numbered thresholds are PROPOSED starting values
from Claude; Jordan can adjust them. Everything else here is binding for all new work.

1. A WORLD BEFORE PIXELS
- Every prefab has written world notes (Foundry_Theme_Bible.md plus edition notes): who runs this world and what the user is
  inside it; its institutions and departments; places; recurring named entities; vocabulary; a one-line emotional register
  (e.g. Halcyon: reassuring surface, unsettling systems); and a taboo list.
- New work starts by extending those notes (new departments, entities, records) before drawing anything. Anything that can't
  be explained inside the world is cut. New canon is written back into the notes the same day.

2. CONSISTENCY TO THEME (IDENTITY IS LOCKED)
- Palette roles, the typography pair, the framing system (headers, corners, rules, grids), icon style and voice are fixed per
  theme/edition. They apply to every surface: 4 lock screens, home, 11 icons, every widget at all three sizes.
- Every label is written in-world. Generic UI words appear only where a real function needs them to be usable (e.g. "OPEN
  YOUTUBE" on a launcher).
- Every object passes "would this exist in this world, made by these people?" Cross-theme bleed is forbidden: no radar or
  contact catalogues outside Halcyon/Deep Range, no organs outside Halcyon, no Win95 chrome outside Vapor Plaza, no botanical
  filler outside Wildwood, no telemetry dashboards as a default skin.
- Inspiration (films, games, books, reference images) supplies visual grammar only. It is translated into the theme's own
  institutions and objects, never reproduced.
- An Expanded/Illustrated edition is the same world seen deeper. It keeps the world's names, departments and voice; it may add
  canon but never contradict the Original's.

3. UNIQUENESS ACROSS THEMES
- The same widget mode in two themes must differ in concept, central graphic family and writing, not just palette.
- Each theme owns exactly one of each, never shared with another theme: idle-game mechanic, oddity concept, gallery subject
  family, Discord gathering metaphor, transmission source structure (who speaks, through what channel).
- PROPOSED check: keep a cross-theme matrix (theme x widget mode -> concept + central graphic family + signature subject) in
  docs/. A design that duplicates any cell of another theme is rejected before review.

4. VARIETY WITHIN A THEME
- Rotating content comes from curated pools of recognizably different subjects: different silhouette, structure, composition
  and short writing. A new name or color on the same drawing is not variety.
- Recurring identities: a named subject keeps its name, look and facts every time it returns, so it becomes familiar.
- Rotation is deterministic per time slot. No subject repeats within one view, neighboring cards differ, and consecutive slots
  never show the same subject.
- Department graphics: no instrument family is the main graphic in more than one widget of the same theme. Secondary
  instruments are chosen per widget from families native to the world (trend, scatter, network, matrix, phase, queue, scope,
  orbit, ledger, contour, gate, envelope, map, specimen record and so on).
- PROPOSED minimums per theme/edition:
  - gallery and catalogue pools: 12+ distinct subjects (Halcyon 36 and Deep Range 21 already pass);
  - transmission: 20+ in-world lines with issuing departments, plus clearly attributed archive quotes;
  - each oddity content field: 10+ entries;
  - idle game: 4+ per-run character variations, plus a persistent save;
  - 4 lock screens that differ in subject and composition inside the approved layout;
  - every home frame genuinely different.
- Small, medium and large are composed separately. Small keeps each subject's distinctive form; detail is reduced only where
  needed for fit, never by default.

5. IMMERSION
- Real utility stays truthful. Calendar, weather, battery and game state come from real adapters. Missing data shows an
  in-world "unavailable" state, never an invented number. Fictional readings are labeled fictional wherever they could be
  mistaken for real (e.g. real planets vs. fictional survey positions).
- Negative space carries the world: serials, custodial tags, department marks, annotations and records. It stays subordinate
  to the primary reading and never obscures it. Empty space must be a deliberate compositional choice.
- Continuity across surfaces: lock, home, icons and widgets share the world's names, IDs, places and departments (the same unit
  number, relay, lot or guild recurs). Icon codes are in-world.
- Time passes in-world. Content changes on refresh at a deliberate pace (normally every 30 minutes). Persistent state (games,
  joining sigils, catalogues) remembers the user's place. Never imply continuous background activity.
- Every line of writing is checked against the theme's one-line register and taboo list before it ships.

6. GATE BEFORE A THEME SHIPS (pass/fail, recorded in this file)
- World notes updated; cross-theme matrix checked.
- Variety sheet for every rotating pool at all three sizes, plus long-name and odd-geometry stress cases.
- Side-by-side comparison with the approved reference and with the Original edition.
- Content-rule scan: no "...", no real CJK, originality, stereotypes, theme taboos.
- Behavior checks (real data, unavailable states, saves, links) and the site-size check.
- Approvals and corrections recorded here, with superseded decisions marked.
