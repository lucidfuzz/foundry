PURPOSE
This is the current design baseline, not a new creative brief. Read it before changing a prefab. Preserve approved work unless the user changes direction. The repeated loss of detail, tone, variety and palette separation caused avoidable rework. Treat those as known regressions to check, not opportunities to ask the user to explain the same preferences again.

REFERENCE PRIORITY
The latest explicit user direction takes precedence. For Halcyon, use the user's Claude widget screenshots for layout, tone, instrument density and palette roles; use the approved detailed organ concepts for the designated organ artwork. Do not use an earlier sparse Codex implementation as the visual target. Marathon references inform the art language; do not recreate its shells or ship designs literally.

HALCYON / TONE AND DENSITY
- A superficially reassuring biofabrication corporation with dark implications in its systems and small print. Synthetic people, human organs and strange synthetic-organic life; not a friendly plant company.
- Technocratic, regulated, bureaucratic and quietly ominous. Avoid overt surveillance eyes, blunt villain imagery and welcoming lifestyle-app layouts.
- Use available negative space for varied fictional tracking, corporate tagging, custodial records, serials, scan annotations and instruments. Protect readable primary content. Do not simplify the approved detail away during implementation or size adaptation.
- Departments need distinct graphics. Repeating one waveform, barcode or tracking graphic across all widgets is insufficient. Keep the approved instrument variety.

HALCYON / ARTWORK AND COLOR
- Heart artwork belongs to Subject Status. Lungs belong to Solar + Atmos. Neural, optical, archive, collective and transmission artwork each stay with their own widget. No repeated hero image subjects across different widgets. Sizes of the same widget retain their subject.
- Preserve the detailed approved organ artwork in production. Replacing it with a generic flat substitute is a regression.
- Observation uses lilac headers and horizon, evergreen field, mint grid/tracks and ice-cyan returns. Palette variation gives contacts character; do not collapse it back to monochrome.
- Retain scan detail: filled vector silhouettes, structure-specific details, spectral readings, measuring rails, colored sectors, contact trails, acquisition gates and horizon mesh. No 3D ship replacement.

HALCYON / WIDGET BEHAVIOR
- Allocation is time allocated by Halcyon. Countdown to the event, elapsed time during its scheduled duration, then countdown to its next matching occurrence. Preserve dystopian corporate framing.
- Audio Channel is heavily regulated corporate radio. Preserve the approved abstract growing seal; abstract imagery carries the unease.
- Transmission cycles attributed literary/archive material and clearly attributed fictional Halcyon statements. Preserve its detailed signal graphic and attribution.
- Specimen is its own abstract bio-synthetic organism growing in a tube while charging. It is not another heart/lung cartridge. Preserve five distinct growth forms, trait variation, saved state and charge-gated growth. Updates are sampled on widget refresh; do not promise continuous background monitoring.
- Distant Observation is a simulated location tracker. A separate Target Dossier below it shows what was scanned. Preserve matching contact/scan IDs and varied object-specific details.
- The approved catalogue contains 36 contacts: six known planets and thirty fictional vessels, structures, organisms and anomalies. Rotate consistently; keep fictional survey positions distinguishable from real telemetry. A new name on the same graphic is not enough variety.

ALL PREFABS / IMMERSION
- Preserve palette, typography, framing and layout identity while varying subjects, silhouettes, structures, telemetry and fictional writing. Users should feel they inhabit a larger world.
- Keep actual calendar/weather/battery readings authoritative. Fictional worldbuilding can be dense without fabricating those practical readings.
- Compose small, medium and large independently. Preserve character and hierarchy at every size; reduce secondary detail only where necessary for fit.
- Concept-to-code fidelity matters. A functioning but visually impoverished sample does not satisfy an approved concept.

DEEP RANGE / PRIOR CORRECTIONS
- Vector instrument art throughout, including ships, planets, widgets and wallpapers. Do not restore the superseded 3D relay/ship.
- Lock variants use the original approved composition: reserved clock space, yellow headline/metadata, inclined tracking plane, red edge marks and year above cobalt horizon. Vary subjects and trajectories within that layout.
- Halcyon lock/home rework remains deferred until the user requests it.

REQUIRED REVIEW BEFORE PRESENTING A DESIGN CHANGE
1. Read these decisions and inspect the relevant original Claude reference alongside the current approved render. Do not rely only on recollection or descriptions.
2. State the requested scope internally and preserve everything else that was approved.
3. Compare actual implementation output against the reference: density, palette roles, tone, graphic variety, image ownership and size consistency.
4. Inspect changed widgets at all three sizes. For rotating content, inspect a contact sheet and stress cases with long names or unusual geometry.
5. Verify the shared preview/export renderer, relevant behavior checks and generated outputs. Record platform limits accurately; a passing code test does not establish visual fidelity.
6. Record new approvals/corrections here and in theme notes. Mark superseded decisions clearly. Include this file in future handoffs.

[ADDED] WORLD-BUILDING STANDARD: EVERY PREFAB, EVERY EDITION
APPROVED intent (Jordan, 12 Oct): "we will be doing this for every single theme, so the rules regarding immersion, world
building, uniqueness, consistency to theme and variety need to be airtight." Numbered thresholds are PROPOSED starting values
from Claude; Jordan can adjust them. Everything else here is binding for all new work.

1. A WORLD BEFORE PIXELS
- Every prefab has written world notes (Foundry_Theme_Bible.md plus edition notes): who runs this world and what the user is
  inside it; its institutions and departments; places; recurring named entities; vocabulary; a one-line emotional register
  (e.g. Halcyon: reassuring surface, unsettling systems); and a taboo list.
- New work starts by extending those notes (new departments, entities, records) before drawing anything. Anything that can't
  be explained inside the world is cut. New canon is written back into the notes the same day.

2. CONSISTENCY TO THEME (IDENTITY IS LOCKED)
- Palette roles, the typography pair, the framing system (headers, corners, rules, grids), icon style and voice are fixed per
  theme/edition. They apply to every surface: 4 lock screens, home, 11 icons, every widget at all three sizes.
- Every label is written in-world. Generic UI words appear only where a real function needs them to be usable (e.g. "OPEN
  YOUTUBE" on a launcher).
- Every object passes "would this exist in this world, made by these people?" Cross-theme bleed is forbidden: no radar or
  contact catalogues outside Halcyon/Deep Range, no organs outside Halcyon, no Win95 chrome outside Vapor Plaza, no botanical
  filler outside Wildwood, no telemetry dashboards as a default skin.
- Inspiration (films, games, books, reference images) supplies visual grammar only. It is translated into the theme's own
  institutions and objects, never reproduced.
- An Expanded/Illustrated edition is the same world seen deeper. It keeps the world's names, departments and voice; it may add
  canon but never contradict the Original's.

3. UNIQUENESS ACROSS THEMES
- The same widget mode in two themes must differ in concept, central graphic family and writing, not just palette.
- Each theme owns exactly one of each, never shared with another theme: idle-game mechanic, oddity concept, gallery subject
  family, Discord gathering metaphor, transmission source structure (who speaks, through what channel).
- PROPOSED check: keep a cross-theme matrix (theme x widget mode -> concept + central graphic family + signature subject) in
  docs/. A design that duplicates any cell of another theme is rejected before review.

4. VARIETY WITHIN A THEME
- Rotating content comes from curated pools of recognizably different subjects: different silhouette, structure, composition
  and short writing. A new name or color on the same drawing is not variety.
- Recurring identities: a named subject keeps its name, look and facts every time it returns, so it becomes familiar.
- Rotation is deterministic per time slot. No subject repeats within one view, neighboring cards differ, and consecutive slots
  never show the same subject.
- Department graphics: no instrument family is the main graphic in more than one widget of the same theme. Secondary
  instruments are chosen per widget from families native to the world (trend, scatter, network, matrix, phase, queue, scope,
  orbit, ledger, contour, gate, envelope, map, specimen record and so on).
- PROPOSED minimums per theme/edition:
  - gallery and catalogue pools: 12+ distinct subjects (Halcyon 36 and Deep Range 21 already pass);
  - transmission: 20+ in-world lines with issuing departments, plus clearly attributed archive quotes;
  - each oddity content field: 10+ entries;
  - idle game: 4+ per-run character variations, plus a persistent save;
  - 4 lock screens that differ in subject and composition inside the approved layout;
  - every home frame genuinely different.
- Small, medium and large are composed separately. Small keeps each subject's distinctive form; detail is reduced only where
  needed for fit, never by default.

5. IMMERSION
- Real utility stays truthful. Calendar, weather, battery and game state come from real adapters. Missing data shows an
  in-world "unavailable" state, never an invented number. Fictional readings are labeled fictional wherever they could be
  mistaken for real (e.g. real planets vs. fictional survey positions).
- Negative space carries the world: serials, custodial tags, department marks, annotations and records. It stays subordinate
  to the primary reading and never obscures it. Empty space must be a deliberate compositional choice.
- Continuity across surfaces: lock, home, icons and widgets share the world's names, IDs, places and departments (the same unit
  number, relay, lot or guild recurs). Icon codes are in-world.
- Time passes in-world. Content changes on refresh at a deliberate pace (normally every 30 minutes). Persistent state (games,
  joining sigils, catalogues) remembers the user's place. Never imply continuous background activity.
- Every line of writing is checked against the theme's one-line register and taboo list before it ships.

6. GATE BEFORE A THEME SHIPS (pass/fail, recorded in this file)
- World notes updated; cross-theme matrix checked.
- Variety sheet for every rotating pool at all three sizes, plus long-name and odd-geometry stress cases.
- Side-by-side comparison with the approved reference and with the Original edition.
- Content-rule scan: no "...", no real CJK, originality, stereotypes, theme taboos.
- Behavior checks (real data, unavailable states, saves, links) and the site-size check.
- Approvals and corrections recorded here, with superseded decisions marked.
